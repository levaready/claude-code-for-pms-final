## What the table says
- **4 responders have collapsed in the CSV:** Farlight, Meteor Mite, The Undertow, Vesper. Only 2 of them (Farlight, The Undertow) have tickets. Meteor Mite and Vesper surfaced only in interviews.
- **16 of the 25 tickets, from 9 responders, describe quiet stretches the CSV does not show.** Those responders (Ashgrove, Cindermark, Halfmoon, Ironvale, Nightwell, Falkirk, Stormwrack, The Drift, The Longcast) still receive and accept offers at roughly their old rate or higher. 3 of the 16 (T-011, T-020, T-025) also describe a lost offer, which the CSV cannot test.
- **5 tickets are lost-offer only** (T-001, 003, 007, 015, 023). The weekly CSV has no timing data, so it can neither confirm nor refute them. The interviews add two more lost-offer accounts (Dot for Vesper, Halloran for Bulwark, whose volume is normal) and repeat T-001 (Ambrose).
- Ticket volume is therefore a poor guide to who is affected. The most affected responders (Vesper, Meteor Mite) filed nothing.

## Caveats
- The CSV is weekly, so it cannot test "since the 12th" or "six days". The contradictions above are cases where the CSV shows 5 or more accepted offers in a week the ticket calls empty.
- Provenance and the definition of `pings_taken` are unknown. If it is not "offers accepted", the CSV comparison changes.
- The tickets are 25 files; I don't know whether that is the full set or a sample.
- Nothing after 31 Aug in the CSV, so the latest ticket claims (up to 5 Sep) are compared against older data.
