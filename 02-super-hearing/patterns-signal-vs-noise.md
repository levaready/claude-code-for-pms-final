## What ties these together

The biggest risk right now is chasing the loudest signal instead of the largest one. Row 2 says the tickets are noisy; row 7 says the interviews are a non-representative sample. Row 1 says the CSV, which is quieter than both, points at something worse: total silence for four responders, and even the two piles combined only give a qualitative account for half of them. Row 4 closes off the easy explanation everyone (including Marcus and Priya) reaches for first, and row 8 replaces it with a better-grounded one: the four aren't just declining, they're caught in a loop the routing code has no mechanism to break. Severity is also subjective across sources (row 3) — one calm handler shouldn't be read as evidence nothing's wrong. That leaves rows 1, 3 and 8 as what's actually worth Wen and Ravi's time next, and all three need real per-responder data to resolve, not more interviews.

**The one number for Helen:** 1 in 4 responders (4 of 16) are now getting offered work about 80% less often than before 4.2 — lead with this over the acceptance rate, which is recovering (73% and climbing) and tells the opposite story.

Of the six, **row 2 is the cheapest to move and the highest-leverage**: one clarifying question to Ravi (what `pings_taken` measures, and whether it reconciles with what handlers see) settles a dependency every other row sits on.
