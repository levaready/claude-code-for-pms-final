"Back to normal" means getting at least half of their own usual offers, and at least 3, every week from then to the end.

Who pays for the fix?

Offers are shared out, so when a left-out hero gets offers back, other heroes usually get fewer. A longer time limit also means fewer offers get passed down the line. This follows the 4 heroes carrying the most extra load when the fix starts. It shows their average offers a week.

What stands out in this run

    Assumptions (what is real and what is made up)

    Taken from the real code

    • Each hero has a score that sets their place in line. It starts at 0.5, goes down 0.12 for a no or a timeout (they count the same), and goes up 0.08 for a yes. It stays between 0 and 1.
    • Nothing moves the score back on its own. It only changes when a hero is offered a job and answers.
    • Before 4.2: 90 seconds, and the line is set by distance 0.45, recent yeses 0.40, skills 0.15. From 4.2: 60 seconds, 0.60, 0.25, 0.15.
    • An incident offers the job to heroes down the line, one at a time, and stops at the first yes. Each gets up to the time limit.

    Made up for this model (we do not know these)

    • How long each hero usually takes to answer. We spread them from 10 to 66 seconds, so several are near the 60 to 90 second zone. Answer times vary a bit from offer to offer.
    • How far each hero is from each incident, which heroes are free for an incident (40 in 100), and which skills a job needs.
    • About 150 incidents a week, which gives about 165 offers a week, close to the real total. Heroes say yes 90 to 99 percent of the time when they have time to answer.
    • The 16 heroes here are made-up heroes. They are not the real four. Which ones get left out comes from the made-up run.
    • The model runs 20 weeks, from 29 Jun to 9 Nov. Weeks after 31 Aug are made up, since we don't have that data yet.
    • In the real data, the four left-out heroes did not recover in the weeks we have. In this model some recover on their own by luck, even when we do nothing. So the model may make the problem look easier to climb out of than it really is.
    • Option D's one-time lift only moves scores below 0.5 up to 0.5. Option C's recovery also only lifts low scores.

    Not checked

    • Whether the real routing works exactly like the code files say. We have one commit, after 4.2, so we cannot compare against the old code.
    • Whether timeouts are logged as no answer or as a decline. The code scores them the same either way.
    • What this means for Supply. Supply is the team that books gear maintenance, and it reads the availability record that Dispatch writes. A one-time lift or a recovery rule could change what Supply sees, so we would check with them first.
    • We have not asked Wen. The model has a lot of noise, so each number is the average of 10 made-up runs, and the charts show only the first run. Real life will differ.
